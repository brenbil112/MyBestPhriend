Oncology Resources

Clinical Pharmacology and Facts & Comparisons - Useful for drug-specific information such as side effects and administration recommendations. Can also find some breakdowns for many common chemotherapy regimens on ClinPharm. For emetic potential go to ‘administration section’.

UpToDate - Great for researching disease state information as well as specific treatment regimens and guidelines. Has useful PDFs for many chemotherapy regimens.

ChemoCare.com - Useful for patient information, helps put side effects and considerations into easier-to-understand language for patients. ***While reviewed by Cleveland Clinic team, note that site does not cite resources, and some useful information for clinicians may be omitted.

National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) - Must sign up for free account to access, can also get JCCN magazine sign-up as well. Provides information around treatment guidelines based on disease state for providers, as well as disease-state specific information for patients. However there is limited drug information available and large amount of clinical data to parse through.

HemeOnc.org - A wiki with information regarding all things hematology/oncology. Has information regarding treatment regimens, has links to ‘example orders’ that contain common pre-medications. ***While this site contains links to any studies referenced, bear in mind that it is a Wiki and user-edited so any information provided should be validated before being utilized.

Oncology Nursing Society - Can search for drug information sheets, has nurse-focused drug information guides for many common medications with attached references. ***These guides are directed to provide basic information to nurses and do not list all side effects or considerations for each medication

Oncology Drugs Quick Reference

Pembrolizumab (Keytruda)

Author: BB

Class: MAB, Anti-PD-1

Common Pre-treatments

None usually; may use acetaminophen or antihistamine if mild/moderate reaction occurred previously.

Emetic Risk

Low

Side Effects

Fatigue, musculoskeletal pain, skin reactions, infusion reactions, thyroid disorders, electrolyte abnormalities, hyper/hypoglycemia, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, elevated liver enzymes, nausea/vomiting.

Sources

ClinicalKey, UpToDate

Oxaliplatin

Author: BB

Class: Alkylating agent, platinum

Common Pre-treatments

Depends on regimen and patient reaction; may include anti-emetics and corticosteroids.

Emetic Risk

Moderate

Side Effects

Peripheral neuropathy, cold sensitivity, diarrhea, injection site reactions, skin disorders, elevated LFTs/creatinine, fatigue, nausea/vomiting, infection risk, electrolyte abnormalities.

Clinical Pearls

  • Calcium/magnesium infusions have not shown consistent benefit in preventing neuropathy.
  • Patients should avoid cold exposure and ice-cold drinks.
  • Cooling/icing during infusions has unclear evidence.

Sources

ClinicalKey, UpToDate

Atezolizumab (Tecentriq)

Author: BB

Class: MAB, Anti-PD-L1

Common Pre-treatments

None usually; may use acetaminophen or antihistamine if prior reaction.

Emetic Risk

Low

Side Effects

Cough, pneumonitis/ILD, constipation, abdominal pain, nausea/vomiting, thyroid dysfunction, glycemic abnormalities, edema, electrolyte abnormalities, hypertension.

Sources

ClinicalKey, UpToDate

Paclitaxel (Taxol)

Author: BB

Class: Taxane

Common Pre-treatments

Dexamethasone, diphenhydramine, famotidine.

Emetic Risk

Low to Moderate

Side Effects

Bone marrow suppression, neutropenia, anemia, alopecia, myalgias/arthralgias, fever, hypersensitivity reactions, peripheral neuropathy, diarrhea, nausea/vomiting, hepatic/renal dysfunction.

Clinical Pearls

One of the most likely chemotherapy agents to cause hair loss.

Sources

ClinicalKey, UpToDate

Cyclophosphamide (Cytoxan)

Author: BB

Class: Alkylating agent (nitrogen mustard)

Common Pre-treatments

Corticosteroids, anti-emetics, pre- and post-hydration.

Emetic Risk

Moderate to High (dose/regimen dependent)

Side Effects

Bone marrow suppression, infection risk, urotoxicity including hemorrhagic cystitis, renal failure, cardiotoxicity, hyponatremia, pulmonary toxicity, GI upset, alopecia.

Clinical Pearls

  • Patients should remain adequately hydrated.
  • High likelihood of hair loss.

Sources

ClinicalKey

Datopotamab Deruxtecan (Datroway)

Author: BB

Class: Trop-2 antibody and topoisomerase inhibitor conjugate

Common Pre-treatments

Antihistamine, acetaminophen, anti-emetic, dexamethasone mouthwash.

Emetic Risk

High

Side Effects

Nausea/vomiting, constipation, diarrhea, alopecia, ocular toxicity, stomatitis, pneumonitis/ILD, anorexia, anemia/leukopenia.

Clinical Pearls / Other Notes

  • Dexamethasone mouthwash used prophylactically for stomatitis.
  • Lubricant eye drops and routine eye exams recommended.

Sources / More Information

ClinicalKey

Etoposide (VP-16)

Author: BB

Class: Topoisomerase inhibitor

Common Pre-treatments

Depends on regimen and patient reaction.

Emetic Risk

Low (IV and oral)

Side Effects

Bone marrow suppression, hypersensitivity reactions, nausea/vomiting, diarrhea, anorexia, hypotension, hepatic/renal dysfunction, alopecia.

Clinical Pearls / Other Notes

  • Oral formulation available for select SCLC cases.
  • Etoposide phosphate may be better tolerated.

Sources / More Information

ClinicalKey

Nivolumab / Relatlimab (Opdualag)

Author: BB

Class: PD-1 antibody and LAG-3 antibody combination

Common Pre-treatments

None usually; may give if prior reactions occurred.

Emetic Risk

Low

Side Effects

Diarrhea, cough, fatigue, musculoskeletal pain, rash, nausea, decreased appetite, thyroid dysfunction, hyperglycemia, renal/hepatic dysfunction, anemia.
