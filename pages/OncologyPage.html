Oncology Drugs Quick Reference

Pembrolizumab (Keytruda)

Author: BB

Class: MAB, Anti-PD-1

Common Pre-treatments

None usually; may use acetaminophen or antihistamine if mild/moderate reaction occurred previously.

Emetic Risk

Low

Side Effects

Fatigue, musculoskeletal pain, skin reactions, infusion reactions, thyroid disorders, electrolyte abnormalities, hyper/hypoglycemia, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, elevated liver enzymes, nausea/vomiting.

Sources

ClinicalKey, UpToDate

Oxaliplatin

Author: BB

Class: Alkylating agent, platinum

Common Pre-treatments

Depends on regimen and patient reaction; may include anti-emetics and corticosteroids.

Emetic Risk

Moderate

Side Effects

Peripheral neuropathy, cold sensitivity, diarrhea, injection site reactions, skin disorders, elevated LFTs/creatinine, fatigue, nausea/vomiting, infection risk, electrolyte abnormalities.

Clinical Pearls

  • Calcium/magnesium infusions have not shown consistent benefit in preventing neuropathy.
  • Patients should avoid cold exposure and ice-cold drinks.
  • Cooling/icing during infusions has unclear evidence.

Sources

ClinicalKey, UpToDate

Atezolizumab (Tecentriq)

Author: BB

Class: MAB, Anti-PD-L1

Common Pre-treatments

None usually; may use acetaminophen or antihistamine if prior reaction.

Emetic Risk

Low

Side Effects

Cough, pneumonitis/ILD, constipation, abdominal pain, nausea/vomiting, thyroid dysfunction, glycemic abnormalities, edema, electrolyte abnormalities, hypertension.

Sources

ClinicalKey, UpToDate

Paclitaxel (Taxol)

Author: BB

Class: Taxane

Common Pre-treatments

Dexamethasone, diphenhydramine, famotidine.

Emetic Risk

Low to Moderate

Side Effects

Bone marrow suppression, neutropenia, anemia, alopecia, myalgias/arthralgias, fever, hypersensitivity reactions, peripheral neuropathy, diarrhea, nausea/vomiting, hepatic/renal dysfunction.

Clinical Pearls

One of the most likely chemotherapy agents to cause hair loss.

Sources

ClinicalKey, UpToDate

Cyclophosphamide (Cytoxan)

Author: BB

Class: Alkylating agent (nitrogen mustard)

Common Pre-treatments

Corticosteroids, anti-emetics, pre- and post-hydration.

Emetic Risk

Moderate to High (dose/regimen dependent)

Side Effects

Bone marrow suppression, infection risk, urotoxicity including hemorrhagic cystitis, renal failure, cardiotoxicity, hyponatremia, pulmonary toxicity, GI upset, alopecia.

Clinical Pearls

  • Patients should remain adequately hydrated.
  • High likelihood of hair loss.

Sources

ClinicalKey
